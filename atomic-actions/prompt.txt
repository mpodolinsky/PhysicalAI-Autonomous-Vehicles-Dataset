You are analyzing a single dashcam-style image taken from an ego vehicle's
front-facing camera, overlaid with a projection of the vehicle's own actual
future trajectory over the next 6.4 seconds.

## The trajectory overlay

- The overlay is a fixed window of exactly 6.4 seconds of ego motion, sampled
  at 10 Hz, i.e. up to 64 dots.
- Color encodes time: the first dot (start of the window, "now") is DARK
  BLUE/PURPLE, passing through GREEN in the middle of the window, and ending
  in YELLOW at the end of the 6.4s window. So reading dark blue -> green ->
  yellow along the dots tells you the order and pacing of the motion.
- Not every dot is necessarily visible: a dot is only drawn if that point in
  space projects inside the visible camera image and in front of the camera.
  If the dots stop partway (fewer than 64 shown, or they end well before the
  image edge/horizon), this can mean EITHER (a) the vehicle actually came to
  a stop around that point (dots cluster and stop advancing in space), OR
  (b) the vehicle kept moving but its future path left the camera's field of
  view (e.g. a sharp turn off-frame, or it went over a hill/dip). Use the
  spacing and clustering of the dots, the vehicle's reported speed/
  acceleration below, and the scene context (intersection, curve, open road,
  stopped traffic ahead, etc.) to judge which case applies -- do not assume
  a short dot trail always means stopping.

## Ego motion at the start of the window

You will also be given the ego vehicle's own instantaneous 3D velocity and
acceleration at the moment the window starts, as `v=(vx,vy,vz) m/s` and
`a=(ax,ay,az) m/s^2` in the vehicle's local frame: x = forward, y = lateral
with POSITIVE y = LEFT and NEGATIVE y = RIGHT, z = vertical (positive up).
Rounded to 1 decimal place, plus total forward speed in km/h. So a positive
vy (or ay) indicates leftward motion (or acceleration), and a negative vy
(or ay) indicates rightward motion (or acceleration) -- do not assume the
opposite convention.

## Your task

Reason step by step about what the dot trail plus the velocity/acceleration
numbers imply about the vehicle's motion over this 6.4s window, then
summarize that motion as a sequence of exactly 3 atomic actions, in order,
each one of:

- `go straight`
- `turn left`
- `turn right`
- `stop`

This vocabulary is deliberately small -- a real 6.4s trajectory (e.g. a
gentle curve, or a rolling stop) will never match it perfectly. Choose the
sequence of 3 actions whose composed path most closely approximates the
actual observed trajectory shape and pacing, e.g. a gentle continuous left
bend over the whole window is best approximated as
`["go straight", "turn left", "turn left"]` or `["turn left", "turn left",
"go straight"]` depending on where the bend is concentrated; a vehicle
braking to a halt partway through is `["go straight", "go straight",
"stop"]`; a vehicle already stopped that starts moving again mid-window
could be `["stop", "go straight", "go straight"]`. Use `stop` only where the
vehicle's own motion is actually (or very likely) stationary, per the
reasoning above -- not merely where the dots run out due to leaving frame.

## Output format

Respond with a single JSON object and nothing else (no markdown code
fences), with exactly these two keys:

```
{
  "reasoning": "<your full step-by-step reasoning trace as free text>",
  "actions": ["<action 1>", "<action 2>", "<action 3>"]
}
```

Each entry in `actions` must be exactly one of "go straight", "turn left",
"turn right", "stop" (lowercase, as written).
